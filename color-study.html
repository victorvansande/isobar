Kwick Weather · design notes

Colour study from your boards

I sampled every reference board pixel by pixel, kept only the saturated colours, and grouped what keeps coming back. Five families carry almost all of it. The khaki, beige and olive tones the first version leaned on mostly came from blurred photos and frosted glass, not from the designed surfaces, so they're out.

02Five families, plus a signal

03How the boards use colour

Four rules I pulled from looking at the cards themselves, not just the swatches.

Bright rim, deep core

The Weather, Veri and Glucose cards are fully saturated at the edges and fall to a dark centre where the number sits. That's what keeps them vivid instead of pastel, and why white type reads on them.

Walk the wheel, never cross it

Blending blue into orange, or green into red, makes mud. That's where the khaki came from. The temperature scale now runs cold to hot through violet and pink, so every in-between step stays clean.

Glass is clear, not tinted

In GCT Balance and Yeasy Run the glass has almost no colour of its own. It picks up whatever sits behind it. So the app's glass cards are near-clear, and a slow field of colour from the current weather drifts underneath them.

04Temperature scale

The hero card and every day in the 14-day stack take their colour from the temperature. Values between stops blend.

05Weather changes the core, not the hue

At the same 15°, the rim stays orchid while the conditions change what's underneath. Night pulls everything toward indigo.

06Where each family lives in the app

Fixed cards get a family that suits their subject. Cards that report a level (UV, air quality) move through families as the level rises.

07Liquid glass

Clear panels with a bright top edge, a light catch on opposite corners and a soft inner glow. The second panel is the day version over the light ground.
